What the filtration labels actually mean

Compare masks: ratings, fit and cost

These are minimum filter efficiencies under each standard’s laboratory tests, not the percentage of protection you will get on your face. Standards use different test conditions; fit and authenticity matter. A “95% BFE” surgical mask is not the same as an N95.

NOT A HAZE RESPIRATOR

Cloth / fashion / paper

No fixed rating

Material and leakage vary. Uncertified “PM2.5” inserts do not establish protection for the whole mask.

Value for haze: avoid spending on these as your particle protection.

LOOSE FIT

Surgical / 3-ply / KF-AD

BFE ≠ respirator rating

Designed mainly for droplets and splashes. A bacterial filtration claim does not tell you how much haze leaks around the edges.

Value for haze: low purchase price does not make it a substitute for a respirator.

PRACTICAL HAZE OPTION

N95

≥95% NIOSH · US

Often uses two headbands. Check the NIOSH name, model and TC approval number. A standard N95 and surgical N95 have the same minimum particle rating.

Value for haze: a useful choice if it seals well; no need to pay for a surgical version solely for haze.

PRACTICAL HAZE OPTION

KN95

≥95% GB 2626 · China

Commonly sold in folding designs. Ear loops or headbands vary by model; verify the stated standard and check for gaps.

Value for haze: compare it with N95 on genuine certification, your fit and price per mask.

PRACTICAL HAZE OPTION

KF94

≥94% MFDS · Korea

Commonly sold in boat shapes. Look for a genuine KF94 product and the correct size. The standard differs from N95, though MOH accepts it for haze.

Value for haze: a practical option if it fits; the one-point rating difference alone should not decide your purchase.

PRACTICAL HAZE OPTION

FFP2

≥94% EN 149 · Europe

A tight-fitting particulate respirator. Check the EN 149 classification and manufacturer’s markings; shape and straps vary.

Value for haze: another suitable category; choose a certified fit rather than paying for an imported label.

Other ratings, reusable respirators and valves
KF80: ≥80%

A lower filtration class. It is not one of MOH’s listed N95-equivalent haze options; select N95, KN95, KF94 or FFP2 when that level of protection is called for.

N99 / FFP3 / KF99: ≥99%

Higher filter ratings with different certification tests. Buying one is optional, not an official requirement triggered by a particular PM2.5 band. A poor seal still lets particles in.

N100 / P100: ≥99.97%

P-series filters also resist oil aerosols. These higher filter classes are not required by MOH’s general haze guide. Balance cost, fit and comfort before upgrading.

Reusable half-face respirators

The fitted filter determines the particle rating. Include replacement filters, cleaning and storage in the cost; follow the manufacturer’s service-life instructions. A gas cartridge alone is not a particulate filter.

Valved vs unvalved

A valve can ease exhalation; it does not increase the stated filter rating. Outward air may bypass filtration, so an unvalved model is the simpler choice when protecting others from respiratory infections also matters.

“Carbon” and “anti-odour” masks

Carbon is not a particle certification. Check for a recognised respirator rating. Particle respirators do not protect against all haze gases or vapours.
