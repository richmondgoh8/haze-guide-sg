AT HOME / AIR PURIFIERS

Got purifier? Put it where it counts.

Start with the room you spend the most time in. Bedroom at night, living room when you’re hanging out. One unit in the corridor won’t reliably clean every bedroom.

Give the intake and outlet space

Don’t squeeze it behind the sofa, under a shelf or against curtains. Leave the gaps stated in the manual—not a one-size-fits-all “30 cm”. Keep the clean-air outlet clear.

Floor or table? Follow the model.

Floor units can stay on the floor; tabletop units belong on a stable surface. No need to raise every purifier to face height. If the draft bothers you, redirect it without blocking it.

Only got one? Pick your main room.

Prioritise where a child, older person or someone sensitive to haze spends time. A smaller enclosed room is easier to filter than the whole flat. During poor outdoor air, limit haze coming in—but don’t ignore heat and ventilation.

Room size, fan speed & running costs

Check smoke CADR, not just “HEPA”

CADR tells you how much filtered air the unit delivers. NEA’s haze FAQ recommends smoke CADR in m³/h of at least 3 × room volume in m³. For a 4 × 3 × 2.6 m room, that’s about 94 m³/h or more. This is a sizing guide, not a clean-air guarantee.

Open-plan living and dining? Count the connected space. Published CADR is usually measured at top speed; sleep mode delivers less clean air.

Let it work, not just look busy

Run it consistently while the room needs filtering, at a speed you can tolerate. Higher speeds filter more air. Check replacement-filter prices and power use too; don’t wash a HEPA filter unless the manual permits it. Avoid ozone-generating cleaners.

A purifier does not supply oxygen or remove CO₂. Its sensor is not a reading for the whole flat, and this page’s outdoor reading cannot tell you your indoor air is safe.
