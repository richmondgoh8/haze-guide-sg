AT HOME

Windows can open or not?

“Close all day, then no oxygen how?”

A typical home isn’t airtight; some air still comes in through gaps. But keeping a room closed for too long can build up heat, moisture, CO₂ and other indoor pollutants. So yes, ventilation still matters during haze.

A fan moves air around. A typical split-unit air-con cools and recirculates room air. A HEPA purifier removes particles. None of these, on its own, brings in fresh outdoor air or removes CO₂.

Haze getting worse? Keep most of it out.

Keep windows and outside doors mostly closed while outdoor air is poor. Use air-con for cooling and a suitable purifier for particles, if available. Keep indoor smoke down too: skip candles, smoking and heavy frying.

Air improves a bit? Use that window.

Check the latest reading and air the room when outdoor air improves, even temporarily. You do not have to wait for a magic “all clear”. The amount of airing needed depends on the room and people inside; a regional reading cannot set a safe timer.

Hazy all day, no air-con? Don’t tahan the heat.

If it is too hot to stay with windows closed, spend time in a cooler indoor place with adequate ventilation if possible. If you must open windows to cool your home, close them once the room is comfortable, then run a purifier if available. Opening lets haze in too, so aim to manage both heat and smoke exposure.

For longer spells: filtered fresh air

NEA also describes filtered mechanical ventilation: bringing outdoor air through a suitable particle filter. It needs proper design and installation. An ordinary fan, recirculating air-con or standalone purifier does not do this by itself.

The card is a starting point, not a 24/7 closure rule. At 56 µg/m³ and above, it suggests limiting haze entry while planning ventilation; in the Normal band, it suggests considering airing. NEA does not set an official one-hour opening cutoff. This page does not measure your indoor PM2.5, CO₂ or temperature. A clear sky or lack of smoke smell does not prove the air is clean.
